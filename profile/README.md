## What jamovi Brings to Statistical Analysis Workflows

jamovi provides a graphical environment for statistical analysis and research data workflows. Its interface is designed to make common statistical procedures accessible while keeping datasets, analysis settings, and results organized within the same project.

Data analysis begins with a suitable dataset. Variables can be reviewed, organized, and prepared before selecting an analytical procedure, helping researchers establish a clear relationship between their research question and the statistical method being used.

Descriptive statistics provide an important starting point for understanding data. Measures such as central tendency, variability, frequencies, and distributions can help reveal the basic characteristics of a dataset before more advanced testing is performed.

Correlation analysis can be used to examine associations between variables. The resulting statistics should be interpreted according to the selected method, measurement characteristics, and research design rather than being treated as evidence of causation.

Reliability analysis is useful when working with multi-item scales and questionnaires. It can help researchers examine whether related items demonstrate a suitable level of internal consistency for the intended measurement context.

Confirmatory factor analysis can support research involving latent constructs and measurement models. It provides a structured way to evaluate whether observed variables correspond with a proposed factor structure.

Cluster analysis offers another approach for exploring patterns within datasets. Suitable clustering methods can help identify groups of observations based on selected characteristics, provided that the resulting groups are interpreted carefully.

Path analysis can be used to represent relationships among multiple variables within a specified analytical model. A clearly defined theoretical framework is important when selecting variables and interpreting the resulting estimates.

Power analysis can help researchers think about sample-size requirements and the ability of a planned statistical procedure to detect effects under specified assumptions. It is best treated as part of study planning rather than as a guarantee of a particular outcome.

Meta-analysis workflows can bring results from multiple studies together for structured quantitative synthesis. Careful study selection, effect-size preparation, and methodological assessment remain important parts of any evidence-synthesis project.

jamovi can also support statistical learning and teaching. Its graphical interface allows students to inspect datasets, select procedures, review results, and connect statistical concepts with practical examples.

The software can be useful across psychology, education, behavioral research, social sciences, and other areas where structured statistical analysis is required. The appropriate procedure depends on the research question, data structure, assumptions, and study design.

For reliable research workflows, data preparation should be separated from interpretation. Checking missing values, variable types, unusual observations, and measurement scales before analysis can help reduce avoidable mistakes.

Results should also be interpreted in context. Statistical output is most useful when combined with the research question, study design, effect estimates, uncertainty measures, and relevant substantive knowledge.

## Starting a jamovi Analysis Session

Prerequisites: Prepare a structured dataset, identify the research question, review the variables and measurement levels, and determine which statistical procedure is appropriate.

1. **Install or Open the Tool:** Start jamovi and open an existing project or begin a new analysis workspace.
2. **Import the Data:** Load the prepared dataset and verify variable names, data types, missing values, and measurement settings.
3. **Explore the Dataset:** Review descriptive statistics and distributions before selecting more advanced procedures.
4. **Choose the Analysis:** Select a statistical method that matches the research question, study design, and characteristics of the data.
5. **Review the Results:** Examine estimates, uncertainty measures, test statistics, effect information, and relevant diagnostic output.
6. **Save and Maintain:** Preserve the analysis project and document important decisions so the workflow can be reviewed later.
